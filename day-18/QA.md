# Day 18 — QA and security review

**Contracts:** `contracts/HimalFlashLender.sol` (ERC-3156 flash lender, 0.09% fee), `contracts/FlashArbitrage.sol` (flash-loan arbitrage bot between two exchanges), `contracts/bonus/SafeBonus.sol` (a loyalty bonus that reads balances at a past block), `contracts/HimalVotesToken.sol` (HIM with ERC20Votes history) · **Reused unchanged from Day 17:** `HimalPair`, `HimalFactory`, `HimalRouter`, `HimalToken` · **Vulnerable on purpose, for the lesson:** `contracts/bonus/NaiveBonus.sol`, `contracts/attack/BonusAttacker.sol` · **Test helpers:** `contracts/test/` (`TestBorrower` behaves well or badly on request, `NaiveBorrower` forgets the initiator check, `FeeToken` takes 1% per transfer) · **Security focus:** using flash loans to attack other contracts

## How it was checked

1. Unit tests (`npx hardhat test`): `maxFlashLoan` and `flashFee` (0.09%, rounded up), a 1,000,000 HUSD loan repaid with exactly 900 HUSD more, lending the whole balance but not 1 wei more, unsupported tokens and zero amounts, owner-only admin, the arbitrage paying exactly the quoted profit, the bonus paying exactly 1 HUSD per 10 HIM held at the snapshot, once.
2. Attack-style tests:
   - **Bad borrowers** that keep the money, approve only the amount, push the money back with `transfer()`, return a wrong value, re-enter `flashLoan`, or use a fee-on-transfer token. Each one reverts and the lender's balance is unchanged.
   - **The flash-loan attack on another contract:** `BonusAttacker` borrows 100,000 HIM, claims from `NaiveBonus`, repays 100,090 HIM and walks away with the whole 10,000 HUSD pot. The same contract against `SafeBonus` reverts.
   - **Attacks on the borrower:** calling `onFlashLoan` directly, and a stranger starting a flash loan *for* the bot. Plus the same against a bot without the initiator check, which loses 900 HUSD of savings per loan.
3. Fuzz tests with **fast-check**: 40 random runs of up to 15 actions: flash loans by a borrower in any of the 6 modes, of either token, for 0% to 105% of what the lender holds, and arbitrage runs of 1 to 600,000 HUSD with `minProfit` from 50% to 150% of the quote. After every action: a loan succeeds **only** for a well-behaved borrower asking for 1 wei up to the full balance; the arbitrage succeeds exactly when the quote is profitable enough, and pays exactly the quote; the lender's balance and `feesEarned` grow by exactly the fee (or not at all), never down; the bot holds 0 of both tokens. Also run once with 400 runs (`npm run fuzz`): green.
4. Mutation check (`npm run mutate`): 17 important lines broken on purpose, one at a time: callback answer not checked, repayment not pulled, balance not re-checked, fee rounds down, no fee at all, `flashLoan` not `nonReentrant`, no maximum-amount check, unsupported tokens lendable, `withdraw` open to anyone, lender not checked, initiator not checked, `minProfit` ignored, profit left in the bot, anyone can run the bot, bonus reads today's balance, snapshot may be in the future, claim twice. The tests caught all 17.
5. `npm audit --omit=dev`, `solhint` (recommended rules), `slither`.
6. Manual review like an attacker: every contract that reads a balance, a price or a vote "right now" can be fooled for one transaction; who can call the callback, and who can start a loan; re-entering during the loan; rounding; odd tokens; leftover allowances; the bot losing money on a bad trade.

## Findings

F-01 to F-06 came from the attacker review of the design before the first build (each is written as a test, and each fix is also proven by a mutation). F-07 was found by slither in review round 2, after the first build was green. F-08 came from the same round.

| F-01 | High | Design review | **A flash loan makes anyone look rich for one transaction.** `NaiveBonus` pays 1 HUSD per 10 HIM using `balanceOf(msg.sender)` *right now*. The attacker, holding 0 HIM at the snapshot, borrows 100,000 HIM, claims, and repays. Result: the whole **10,000 HUSD** pot for a **90 HIM** fee (about 900 HUSD); Ram, a real holder, gets nothing. | `SafeBonus` reads `getPastVotes(user, snapshotBlock)` from the ERC20Votes history, at a block that was already mined when it was deployed. | F-06 | Medium | Design review | **Arbitrage that loses money.** Prices move between sending and mining (another bot may take the gap first), and a bigger loan is not always better: borrowing 200,000 HUSD would *lose* 50,798.90 HUSD. A bad run costs only gas. | `if the profit is below minProfit, everything reverts… (F-06)`, `a trade that would lose money reverts… (F-06)`, fuzz "arbitrage succeeds exactly when profitable enough", mutation "minProfit ignored" |
| F-07 | Low | Review round 2 (slither) | **State written after external calls.** The first build updated `feesEarned` after the callback, and both bonuses emitted `Claimed` after the transfer. Not exploitable here (`nonReentrant`, and a failed loan reverts everything), but it breaks checks-effects-interactions. | `feesEarned` and the `FlashLoan` event are written before any external call; `Claimed` is emitted before the transfer. | full suite still green, fuzz checks `feesEarned` grows by exactly the fee after every action |

Also checked and fine: a plain wallet cannot be a receiver; `maxFlashLoan` is 0 for unsupported tokens and `flashFee` reverts for them; switching a token off works; the bot checks that its two paths form a loop (`BadPath`); every allowance the bot gives is exactly what will be pulled (`forceApprove`), so nothing is left approved; real holders claim once, exactly 1 HUSD per 10 HIM, and HIM bought after the snapshot does not count; the bonus never pays more than its pot.

`slither` reported 0 results on the new contracts. The Day 17 contracts (reused unchanged), the vulnerable-on-purpose `NaiveBonus` / `BonusAttacker` and the test helpers are excluded in `slither.config.json`. On the Day 17 pair, this newer slither (0.11.6) reports 2 reentrancy notes on `swap` and `burn`; both functions are `nonReentrant` and were covered by Day 17's tests and mutations. Reviewed and silenced inline with a reason: `arbitrary-send-erc20` on the lender pulling repayment from the receiver (that is ERC-3156: the receiver just got the loan, approved it and answered the callback; the borrower-side risk is F-02), `reentrancy-balance` on the before/after balance check (it is the point of the check, and `nonReentrant` guards it), `unused-return` on the bot's swaps (it measures real balances instead), `reentrancy-events` on the bot's `Arbitrage` event, and `incorrect-equality` on two `amount == 0` checks.

No high or medium issues remain open.

## Known issues (by design, documented)

- **The lender is owner-funded.** There are no depositors or shares; a real pool (Aave, Balancer) shares fees with liquidity providers.
- **SafeBonus needs delegation.** Only HIM that was delegated (usually to yourself) at the snapshot counts, and votes delegated *to* you count as yours. Holders who never delegated get nothing; a wallet must tell them to delegate before the snapshot.
- **Arbitrage is a race.** Anyone can see the price gap; the first bot to land wins and the others hit `NotProfitable` and pay gas. Real bots use private relays. The demo finds the best size by searching in steps of 1,000 HUSD, off-chain.
- **Fee-on-transfer and rebasing tokens are not supported by the lender.** They fail safely with `NotRepaid`.
- **`NaiveBonus` and `BonusAttacker` are vulnerable / offensive on purpose**, kept only to prove the attack. Never deploy them.
