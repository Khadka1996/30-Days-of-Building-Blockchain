# Day 17 — QA and security review

**Contracts:** `contracts/HimalPair.sol` (one x × y = k pool with LP tokens), `contracts/HimalFactory.sol` (one pool per token pair), `contracts/HimalRouter.sol` (multi-hop swaps, liquidity, `bestPath`), `contracts/HimalToken.sol` (HIM, HUSD, NPRT) · **Test helpers:** `contracts/test/` (`FeeToken` takes 1% per transfer, `HookToken` calls back on every transfer like ERC-777) · **Security focus:** slippage, sandwich attacks

## How it was checked

1. Unit tests (`npx hardhat test`): pool creation and lookups, the first deposit (√(x·y) shares, 1,000 locked), the k check to the wei (the formula's amount works, 1 wei more reverts), every input check on the pair, gifts / `skim` / `sync`, the router maths (`getAmountOut` rounds down, `getAmountIn` rounds up), `bestPath` (two hops for a big trade, direct for a tiny one, empty when no route), exact-in and exact-out swaps over one and two hops, adding at the pool ratio, removing with minimums, deploy checks.
2. Attack-style tests:
   - **A real sandwich in one block:** automining is switched off, the attacker sends a buy with a 100 gwei tip, Ram's swap goes with 2 gwei and the attacker's sell with 1 gwei, then one block is mined. The test checks all three are in the same block, in that order, then compares Ram's result with and without a slippage limit.
   - A swap that waited 2 hours, adding liquidity after the price was pushed, a fee-on-transfer token on both sides, re-entering a pool through a token callback, a huge gift plus `sync`.
3. Fuzz tests with **fast-check**: 40 random runs of up to 20 actions (exact-in and exact-out swaps over 8 routes of 1 or 2 hops, adds and removes by 3 people, sizes up to 200,000 tokens). After every action: each pool's balances equal its reserves, no pool is ever emptied, the router holds 0 of every token, every successful swap delivers **exactly** the quote, and k never falls on any pool a swap touched. The only refusal allowed is "the pool is too small" (no panic, no k failure). At the end everyone removes all liquidity and no token was created or destroyed. Also run once with 400 runs (`npm run fuzz`): the first 400-run attempt stopped at run 120 because a trader tried an exact-out buy from the thin pool that cost 12.75 million NPRT while holding 8.66 million. That was a test-harness gap, not a contract bug (the token correctly refused), so "you cannot afford it" was added to the allowed refusals. Re-run: green.
4. Mutation check (`npm run mutate`): 16 important lines broken on purpose, one at a time: no fee in the k check, k check removed, no locked minimum liquidity, swap not `nonReentrant`, prices from raw balances, burn rounds up, no reserve cap, deadline ignored, slippage limit ignored, router trusts the maths instead of the balance, `getAmountIn` rounds down, `getAmountOut` ignores the fee, add-liquidity minimum ignored, remove-liquidity minimum ignored, `bestPath` ignores connectors, token order mixed up on remove. The tests caught all 16.
5. `npm audit --omit=dev`, `solhint` (recommended rules), `slither`.
6. Manual review like an attacker: sandwiching, stale transactions, price-impact maths, rounding, k-check overflow, first-depositor tricks, gifts to the pool, odd tokens, re-entrancy, routing through pools someone else controls, stuck funds.

## Findings

F-01 to F-04 and F-06 to F-08 came from the attacker review of the design before the first build (each is written as a test, and each fix is also proven by a mutation). F-05 was found in review round 2, after the first build was green.

| ID | Severity | Found by | Description | Fix | Proving test |
|----|----------|----------|-------------|-----|--------------|
| F-01 | High | Design review | **Sandwich attack.** A swap with no slippage limit accepts any price. The attacker sees Ram's 50,000 HUSD swap waiting, buys first with a higher tip, lets Ram buy at the pushed price, then sells straight after, all in one block. Ram got **3,325.27 HIM instead of 4,748.03 (29.97% less)**; the attacker kept **14,425.55 HUSD**. | Every swap takes `amountOutMin` (exact in) or `amountInMax` (exact out). The router reverts with `InsufficientOutputAmount(received, minimum)` / `ExcessiveInputAmount(needed, maximum)`. | F-02 | Medium | Design review | **Stale transactions.** A swap can sit in the queue (low gas, a congested network, or a validator holding it) and run hours later, when the price has moved far, still inside a limit that was set for the old price. | Every router function takes a `deadline` and reverts with `Expired` once `block.timestamp` passes it. | `deadline: a swap that waited 2 hours in the queue refuses to run at the new price (F-02)`, mutation "deadline ignored" |
| F-03 | Medium | Design review | **Adding or removing liquidity at a pushed price.** An attacker moves the price just before Ram adds liquidity, so Ram deposits at a bad ratio and the attacker trades back against Ram's money. The same works when someone creates a new pool at a silly price before the real first deposit. | `addLiquidity` takes `amountAMin` / `amountBMin` and adds only at the pool's current ratio (extra tokens stay with you); `removeLiquidity` takes minimums too. | `adding liquidity after the price was pushed reverts on the minimum amounts (F-03)`, `add and remove liquidity at the pool ratio, with minimums on the way out too`, mutations "add-liquidity minimum ignored", "remove-liquidity minimum ignored" |
| F-04 | Medium | Design review | **Trusting the maths instead of the balance.** With a token that takes a fee on transfer, the quote says Ram gets X but only 99% of X arrives, so a check on the computed amount passes while Ram is short. | The router measures the recipient's real balance before and after and compares **that** with the minimum. Selling a fee token reverts in the pool's k check, so nobody can pay less than the quote. Such tokens are effectively unsupported, and they fail safely. | `buying a fee-on-transfer token: the router checks what really arrived… (F-04)` (reverts with received 99% vs minimum), `selling a fee-on-transfer token… reverts (F-04)`, mutation "trusts the maths, not the balance" |
| F-05 | Medium | Review round 2 | **Locking a pool with an overflow.** The k check multiplies `(balance × 1000)` by itself. For a token with a huge supply, an attacker could send a giant amount to the pool and call `sync`; from then on every swap would overflow and revert, freezing the pool. | Reserves are capped at 2¹¹² − 1 (`MAX_RESERVE`, as in Uniswap V2), far below the overflow point. `sync` and `mint` beyond it revert with `Overflow`, the gift can be `skim`med away, and swaps keep working. | `a huge gift plus sync cannot push reserves past 2^112 and lock every swap… (F-05)`, mutation "no reserve cap" |
| F-06 | Medium | Design review | **Re-entrancy through token callbacks.** A token that calls back on transfer (ERC-777 style) could call `swap` again while the first swap has paid out but not yet updated its reserves, and trade against the old numbers. | `mint`, `burn`, `swap`, `skim` and `sync` are all `nonReentrant`; reserves are only written after the k check. The router keeps no state at all. | `re-entering the pool in the middle of a swap is blocked (F-06)` (`HookToken` re-enters, `ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall`), mutation "swap not nonReentrant" |
| F-07 | Medium | Design review | **Rounding in the trader's favour.** If `getAmountOut` rounded up, or `getAmountIn` rounded down, or the k check ignored the fee, a trader could take 1 wei too much per swap and repeat it. Rounding up on `burn` would let a leaver take more than their share. | `getAmountOut` rounds down, `getAmountIn` rounds up (+1), the k check includes the 0.3% fee, LP payouts round down. Always in the pool's favour. | `k never goes down… asking 1 wei more than the formula reverts`, `getAmountOut rounds down and getAmountIn rounds up…`, fuzz "k never falls on a swap", mutations "no fee in the k check", "k check removed", "getAmountIn rounds down", "getAmountOut ignores the fee", "burn rounds up" |
| F-08 | Medium | Design review | **Prices from raw balances and the first-depositor trick.** If prices used `balanceOf(pool)`, anyone could move them for free by sending tokens in. If the first depositor's shares were not partly locked, they could shrink the supply to 1 share and make later small deposits round down to 0. | Prices use the stored reserves; gifts change nothing until `sync`. The first deposit locks 1,000 LP at `0x…dEaD` forever, and a first deposit too small to cover it is refused. | `a gift sent straight to the pool does not move the price…`, `first deposit: shares = √(x·y), and 1,000 LP are locked forever`, `a first deposit too small… is refused`, mutations "prices from raw balances", "no locked minimum liquidity" |

Also checked and fine: one pool per pair (duplicates, identical tokens and the zero address revert), paths must be 2–4 tokens and every hop must have a pool (`InvalidPath`, `PairMissing`), a swap cannot pay the pool itself or a token contract (`InvalidTo`), the router refuses the zero address as recipient, each pool pays the next pool directly so the router holds 0 tokens (checked after every fuzz action), exact-out swaps never charge more than `amountInMax`, and removing liquidity maps the two tokens back in the right order.

`slither` reported 0 results on the main contracts (OpenZeppelin and test helpers are excluded in `slither.config.json`). Reviewed and handled: `cache-array-length` in `bestPath`, **fixed** by caching the length; `calls-loop`, excluded because every loop is bounded (a path has at most 4 tokens, at most 5 connectors) and calls only our own factory and its pairs; `incorrect-equality` on four `== 0` checks of amounts and supply, silenced inline (they are zero checks, not balances an attacker can steer); `reentrancy-balance` on the router's before/after balance check, silenced inline, because a re-entering token could only *add* to the recipient's balance, which helps the user, never an attacker, and the pools themselves are `nonReentrant`.

No high or medium issues remain open.

## Known issues (by design, documented)

- **A slippage limit is a ceiling, not a shield.** A sandwich that stays inside the limit still works: with 0.5% the attacker can take up to about 0.5%. Use the tightest limit the trade allows, and private transaction relays for big trades.
- **`bestPath` reads live reserves.** One trade can move them, so it must only be used off-chain to pick a route, and always with an `amountOutMin` below its quote. It looks at the direct pool and one connector hop only.
- **No time-weighted price oracle.** Other contracts must never use these pools' spot price as a price feed (Day 16 showed why). Uniswap V2 and later record a TWAP for that.
- **Fee-on-transfer and rebasing tokens are not supported.** They fail safely (revert) instead of giving wrong amounts.
- **Anyone can create a pool for any token**, including fake tokens with real names. The router only trusts the path you pass; wallets must check token addresses.
- **Connectors are fixed at deploy** (at most 5); there is no owner and nothing to upgrade.
